Topic Description

Sumo is a form of full-contact wrestling that originated in Japan. The first written record of sumo comes from the 700s, but back then it was more of a spiritual performance rather than a sport, with sumo becoming more of a sport in the 1600s. There are still many rituals that have been preserved in sumo, like the throwing of salt to purify the ring. Only men are allowed to compete in professional sumo, however women can compete in amateur sumo along with men. Also, there are no weight divisions in sumo, meaning that the relatively light wrestlers can/will go against much heavier wrestlers.

The rules of sumo are very simple: force your opponent out of the dohyo (the circular ring on which sumo bouts take place) or into touching the ground with a body part that is not the soles of his feet. Now there are illegal moves/fouls that a rikishi (a sumo wrestler) can commit that will cause him to automatically lose the bout, such as striking with a closed fist, hair pulling, jabbing at the eyes, or other things that will generally cause long-lasting harm; a rikishi's mawashi (the loincloth that the wrestlers wear) coming undone during a bout also makes then automatically lose.
